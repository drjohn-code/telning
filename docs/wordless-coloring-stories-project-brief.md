## 1. Summary

### Problem
Kids' colouring books sell in very large numbers, but the market is crowded and full of cheap AI books. Profit per book is small. Parents do not search for "silent book" or "wordless book"; they search for the problem they have ("feelings", "anger", "sharing").

### Solution
A series of **wordless colouring stories**. Each book is one story about one feeling or one skill. The child colours the story, then finishes it on "you decide" pages. A short parent page inside the book, and a free parent guide online, show parents how to talk with the child during the story. Sold on Amazon as a printed book, later as a PDF on our own website.

### Outcome
A small, trusted brand that parents, teachers, speech therapists and child therapists buy again and again. Side income first; a licensable brand if it works.

### 3.8 The TADA method (our methodology)

TADA is the Telning team's own method, developed by our cognitive and behavioural scientists from scientific studies and clinical experience. It draws on dialogic reading (see 7.2), the wordless-book research (7.1), Social Story structure (7.7) and choice-and-consequence thinking. The child does most of the talking and makes the decisions; the parent asks and waits.

How to describe it in public: "TADA is a method developed by the Telning team of cognitive and behavioural scientists, based on scientific studies and clinical experience." Do not call it "proven" or "clinically tested" until Telning has its own study.

| Letter | Step | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T** | **Talk** | Child | Looks at the page and tells what they see. The parent waits and does not explain the picture first. |
| **A** | **Ask** | Parent | One question per page: "What is happening?" "What next?" "How does Pim feel?" "Did this ever happen to you?" Then repeats the child's words and adds one word. |
| **D** | **Decide** | Child | At the fork, two endings sit side by side. The child colours the one they would choose. No wrong answer. |
| **A** | **Act** | Child + parent | The child draws their own ending on the empty page. The parent scans the QR code and sends it. A story card with Pim comes back. |

Rules of TADA:
- One question per page, then silence. The parent talks less than the child.
- Repeat, then add: say the child's words back and add one new word or idea.
- Never correct the child's story; ask "why?" instead.
- The drawing page is the finish. Nothing is said out loud, no celebration page; the story card is the reward.
- TADA is a method name only. It is never part of the brand name, series name or domain.

Where TADA appears: a small "TADA method inside" badge on the cover; the parent page in every book; the parent guide ("The TADA Guide: four steps to get your child talking"); the story card ("A TADA ending by Emma, 5"); the teacher pack ("TADA for the classroom"); hashtag #TADAstory.

## 5. Market facts

### 5.1 Demand (numbers to treat with care)
- "Coloring book for kids" has 100,000+ monthly searches; "adult coloring book" about 80,000+. (kdpeasy.com)
- General kids' topics are saturated: "animals coloring book" shows 50,000+ results. (kdpeasy.com)
- Printable colouring pages get 100,000+ monthly searches on Etsy. (insightagent.app)
- Market-size reports disagree wildly (from $151M to $13.76B for adult colouring). Do not quote them.
- Kids' colouring books are the bigger search market; adult books earn more per copy.
- In 2026 the dominant style is "bold and easy": big, simple shapes, finished fast. Coco Wyo's bold-and-easy books topped the Amazon bestseller chart for weeks.
- Buyers have seen a lot of low-effort AI books; reviews punish them hard.

### 5.2 Money (Amazon KDP, US)
- Royalty: 60% of list price at $9.99 or above; 50% below $9.99 (change made 10 June 2025). Expanded distribution: 40%.
- Royalty formula: (rate x list price) − printing cost.
- Printing cost formula: fixed cost + (page count x per-page cost). Example from KDP: a 300-page black-and-white US paperback = $0.85 + 300 x $0.012 = $4.45. Newer example: $1.00 + 200 x $0.012 = $3.40. Large trim sizes (wider than 6.12 in or taller than 9 in) cost more per page. 8.5 x 8.5 in is a large trim.
- One seller guide: a standard 50-page 8.5 x 11 colouring book costs roughly $2.15 to print.
- Rough result for our book (about 60 pages, large trim, black ink, $9.99): royalty ≈ $5.99 − ~$2.30 ≈ **$3.50–3.80 per copy**. Check with the KDP royalty calculator before pricing.
- Typical seller-guide estimates: kids' book at $6.99 → about $2.17 profit; adult book at $9.99 → about $4.30.
- PDF prices on Etsy: $5–8 for 15–25 pages, $9–15 for 30–50 pages.

### 5.3 Digital vs printed
- Printed is the main market (gift, less screen time, Amazon handles everything).
- Digital is smaller but real; teachers and families like reprintable pages.
- Amazon does not sell PDF downloads, and KDP says colouring books are not suited to Kindle. So: Amazon = printed, our website/Etsy = PDF.

### 5.4 Amazon print quality
- Black lines print clean. Paper is 50–61 lb (74–90 gsm) for black ink, similar to printer paper; markers can bleed through. Reviews complain about thin paper, repeated pages, fewer pages than promised, and off-centre printing.
- Fix: one picture per sheet with a blank back; say so on the cover; state the true picture count; order a proof copy before publishing.

## 7. Science and facts to use (with honest limits)

### 7.1 Wordless books and parent–child talk
- Journal of Child Language study (36 parents, children 3.5–5.5): a wordless book produced more interaction, more parent prompts and feedback, and significantly more child contributions than the same story with text. But the parents' vocabulary and grammar were richer with the text book. → Say "gets your child talking", not "better than normal books".
- Utah State University (Gillam & Boyce): mothers used more complex language and interacted more with a wordless book than with a text book; especially useful for children with developmental disabilities; relevant to speech-language pathologists.
- University of Waterloo (O'Neill): mothers used complex talk (predictions, links to the child's own life) more often with a wordless storybook than with a picture vocabulary book.
- Cambridge (2024): wordless books may produce higher-quality, more abstract ("decontextualised") talk than text-and-picture books for some kinds of adult input; open-ended questions matter.
- Grolig et al. 2020 (Early Childhood Research Quarterly, Max Planck): a low-dose **narrative dialogic reading** programme with wordless picture books improved preschoolers' inferential and literal story comprehension and vocabulary; the inference effect lasted five months. Effects were small and mostly short-term.

### 7.2 The talk method: dialogic reading (Whitehurst)
- PEER: **P**rompt the child to say something → **E**valuate the answer → **E**xpand it with one more word or idea → **R**epeat so the child says it again.
- CROWD prompt types: **C**ompletion, **R**ecall, **O**pen-ended ("what is happening here?"), **W**h-questions (who/what/where/why), **D**istancing (link to the child's own life).
- Goal: the child becomes the storyteller; the adult talks less over time.
- Many randomised trials; results are positive but mixed in size. This is the method the parent guide should teach, in plain words.

### 7.3 Colouring and anxiety
- Carsley & Heath (Journal of Applied School Psychology): 52 children, mean age ~11, coloured a mandala or free-coloured before a spelling test; anxiety fell in both groups; girls benefited only from the mandala, boys more from free colouring.
- Carsley et al. 2015 (150 Canadian children, grades 4–6): both mandala colouring and free drawing before a test reduced test anxiety.
- Carsley & Heath (university students): both colouring conditions reduced test anxiety and increased state mindfulness; a non-colouring control group's anxiety rose.
- Flett et al. 2017 (adults): one week of daily colouring lowered depressive symptoms and anxiety compared with logic puzzles.
- One study found calming music reduced test anxiety more than mandala colouring.
- Limits: small samples, short follow-up, mostly older children and adults. Say "a calming activity" and "colouring before a stressful moment can help", nothing more.

### 7.4 Colouring and fine motor skills
- Many small classroom studies (mostly Indonesian kindergarten action research) report better pencil grip, control and accuracy after structured colouring. Quality is low but the direction is consistent, and occupational therapists use colouring as standard practice.
- Safe claim: "supports pencil control and pre-writing skills".

### 7.5 How children draw and colour at each age (Lowenfeld / Kellogg stages)
| Age | Stage | What it means for our pages |
|---|---|---|
| 2–4 | Scribbling (random → controlled → named) | Not our market. Would need 3–5 mm lines, one object per page. |
| 4–7 | Pre-schematic: first symbols (circle + lines = person), colour choice not realistic, big shapes | Our main market. Bold lines (~1.5–2 mm), one scene, few small areas, plenty of white space. By 5 most children can colour inside lines. |
| 7–9 | Schematic: stable repeated symbols, baselines, more detail | Our upper range. Can handle more elements and finer areas. |
| 4–7 is also described as a "crisis" period in child art; many children stop spontaneous drawing by about 8. | | Keep success easy; never make the page look hard. |

### 7.6 Social-emotional learning (SEL) framework for teachers
- CASEL five competencies: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making.
- Publishers tag SEL books by these five; librarians search by them. Tag each of our books to one or two competencies on the back cover and in the teacher pack.
- Note: SEL is politically contested in some US states (Florida phased it out of schools). Parents across the spectrum still support "life skills". Use "feelings and life skills" language on consumer covers, "SEL / CASEL" only in teacher materials.

### 7.7 Social Stories (Carol Gray) — structure to borrow
- Used since 1991 for autistic children. A Social Story has one title, descriptive sentences (who, what, where, why) at least four times as often as coaching sentences, and three parts (introduction, body, conclusion). Half of all stories should celebrate something the child already does well.
- For us: show mostly *what happens* (descriptive), show one gentle *what to do* (coaching), end calm and positive. The child's own drawn ending is the celebration. Keep it first-person-friendly: Pim is the child.
- Research on Social Stories is positive but hard to evaluate; do not claim our books are Social Stories (it is a trademarked method). Say "inspired by visual story methods used with autistic children".

### 7.8 Honest-claims rule
Allowed: "a calming activity", "gets your child talking", "supports pencil control", "research-informed", "created by a team of cognitive and behavioural scientists" (only while true).
Not allowed: "treats", "cures", "reduces anxiety in your child", "ADHD", "autism therapy", "clinically proven", "science-backed" (overused and over-promises).

### 8.3 Story structure (one situation, one problem, one solution)
A 20–25 picture arc:
1. **Setting** (2–3 pages): where we are, who is there, everything calm. Pim does something it is good at (a positive start, as in Social Stories).
2. **Want** (2 pages): the character wants something.
3. **Problem** (4–5 pages): something goes wrong; the feeling appears and grows. Show the feeling in the body: eyebrows, mouth, posture, size, line shakes.
4. **Try** (4–5 pages): the character tries something that does not work (behaviour behind the feeling).
5. **Help / choice** (3–4 pages): a helper appears or the character pauses; the "you decide" fork happens here.
6. **Fix** (3–4 pages): the chosen action; the result; the feeling changes.
7. **Calm again** (1–2 pages): everyone okay. Then the "you decide" fork and the empty drawing page, which is the child's own ending.

Rules of wordless storytelling:
- Read direction left → right. The character enters from the left, moves right.
- One change per page. The page turn is the "what happens next" gap; use it on purpose.
- Keep the character in a similar position on the page so the child finds it fast.
- Show cause on one page and effect on the next.
- No text in the pictures (no signs, no speech bubbles). Thought bubbles with a picture inside are allowed and useful.
- Every page must be understandable alone by a 4-year-old, and the sequence must be understandable without words by a parent.
- Test: show the pages to someone who has not seen the story and ask them to tell it.

### 8.4 Line and page rules (for ages 4–8)
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Line weight | 4–5 pt at final size (about 1.5–1.8 mm). Industry guidance: 3–6 pt for kids; toddlers 3–5 mm; adults 1–2 pt. Never change line weight inside one book. |
| Line colour | Pure black (#000000). No grey, no shading, no anti-aliased edges in the final PDF. |
| Shapes | Every shape fully closed so colour "cannot escape". |
| Areas | No area smaller than a 5-year-old's crayon tip (about 4 mm). Few tiny areas; most areas large. |

| Margins | Nothing important closer than 0.5 in to the edge; KDP minimum outside margin 0.25 in (0.375 in with bleed); inside/gutter margin 0.375 in for 24–150 pages. |
| Page layout | One picture per sheet, right-hand page; the back of each sheet is blank (bleed-through protection). |
| Variety | Rotate composition: centred vignette, full scene, close-up, wide shot; this makes the Amazon "Look Inside" preview interesting. |

### 8.5 Character design rules (Pim)
- Pim is a round, seed-like creature with a small shoot (two leaves) on top. This links Pim to Telning (sapling) and to the TADA idea (growing) without saying it.
- Simple geometry: round body, 2 dots or 2 circles for eyes, eyebrows, mouth, 2 small arms, 2 feet, one shoot on top. Drawable by code the same way every time.
- Build a reusable SVG component: body, face layer (8 expressions), pose layer (10 poses). Every page uses the same components, so the character never "drifts".
- Emotion sheet (8): calm, happy, surprised, worried, sad, angry, scared, proud.
- Pose sheet (10): standing, walking, sitting, reaching, hiding, hugging, stomping, lying down, pointing, jumping.
- A second simple character (friend/helper) and one adult figure (parent/doctor) built the same way.
- Gender-neutral. Not a known animal shape that reads as a famous character.
- Keep a "model sheet" file and check every page against it.

### 8.7 Parent page inside the book (text, kept short)
- The four TADA steps:
  - **T – Talk** (child): tells what they see on each page. The parent waits and does not explain.
  - **A – Ask** (parent): one question per page: "What is happening?" "What next?" "How does Pim feel?" "Did this ever happen to you?"
  - **D – Decide** (child): at the fork, colours the ending they would choose.
  - **A – Act** (child + parent): the child draws their own ending on the empty page; the parent scans the QR and sends it; the story card comes back.
- Also: look first, no explaining; wait; repeat the child's words and add one word.
- Five questions printed on the page.
- One line: colouring before a stressful moment can calm; the feelings-check page helps the child notice.
- QR + short URL to the full guide.

### 8.8 Feelings-check page
- Five faces (same face style as the character), "before" row and "after" row, child circles one. Simple, no words needed except "before/after" (icons possible).

### 8.9 Production workflow with Claude
1. Story script: a one-line description per page (what we see, what the character feels, what changes).
2. Model sheet: Claude Design / Claude Code builds the SVG components.
3. Pages: Claude Code composes each page from components + simple scene shapes; export SVG.
4. Check each page by eye: closed shapes, line weight, tiny areas, text-free.
5. Assemble PDF at 8.5 x 8.5 in, 300 DPI, correct page order with blank backs (Python: cairosvg/reportlab or similar).
6. Preflight: page count even and ≥ 24, page size exact, fonts embedded (only on text pages), file under 650 MB.
7. Cover: separate PDF with the exact spine width for the final page count.
8. Order a proof copy. Check off-centre printing, line visibility, bleed-through with markers.
9. Upload to KDP with AI disclosure (images = AI-generated, text = as applicable).

## 9. Rules: Amazon KDP

### 9.1 Formatting
- Minimum 24 pages, maximum depends on size and ink; black-ink paper 50–61 lb (74–90 gsm), white or cream; colour-ink paper 60–71 lb.
- 300 DPI minimum; minimum font size 7 pt.
- Bleed 0.125 in each side if used; no-bleed pages are set to exact trim size.
- 8.5 x 8.5 in is a large trim size (anything wider than 6.12 in or taller than 9 in): slightly higher print cost; verify in the calculator.
- Odd page count → KDP adds a blank page.
- Proof and author copies available at print cost + shipping.

### 9.2 Metadata (title, subtitle, keywords)
- Title must be exactly what is on the cover. Title + subtitle under 200 characters together.
- Not allowed in title/subtitle/keywords: "best", "free", "new", "bestseller", "#1", award claims, other authors' names, "Amazon" or "Kindle", unrelated keywords.
- Seven backend keyword boxes: use words not already in the title.
- Series name must follow the same rules as the title.
- Description cannot contain website addresses, emails or phone numbers.
- Metadata that misrepresents the book is actively enforced.

### 9.3 AI content
- AI-generated images or text must be disclosed at upload (checkbox). Disclosure does not block publishing and (per Amazon) does not affect ranking.
- AI-assisted (human-made, AI-helped) does not need disclosure; AI-generated (AI made the first version, even if edited) does.
- Amazon removes AI books that "create a disappointing customer experience".
- Limit: 3 new titles per day per account (not a problem for us).

### 9.4 Links, QR codes, bonus content
- A QR code inside a print book linking to the author's website is allowed and common.
- Do not link to a shop selling the book elsewhere; do not promise "gifts or rewards"; no "disruptive" links.
- Bonus content should be at the end and no more than about 10% of the book.

### 9.5 Royalties and pricing (US)
- 60% at $9.99+; 50% below; expanded distribution 40%; minus printing cost.
- Thresholds in other markets: €9.99, £7.99, CAD/AUD 13.99, SEK 99, PLN 40, JPY 1000.
- Price every book at or above the threshold in each market.

## 10. Rules: legal and safety

| Area | Rule | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| US child product safety (CPSIA) | Books for children 12 and under are "children's products". Ordinary ink-on-paper books are exempt from lead testing. A permanent tracking label (where/when printed, batch) is required on children's books. | KDP prints its own production line on the last page of every book (location, date, code). Keep it; do not cover it. Confirm current KDP guidance before launch. |
| Children's data (GDPR, EU) | Data about children is specially protected; data about a child's mental state is special-category data. | Story-card form: first name + age + parent email only; parental consent checkbox; delete files after 30 days; no photos of children; separate children's privacy notice. No psychological analysis of any child. |
| US children's online privacy (COPPA) | Online collection from children under 13 needs parental consent. | The form is for parents only; state it clearly. |
| Health and medical claims | EU and US rules restrict medical claims; a scientific team credit raises the bar. | Use the honest-claims rule (7.8). No "treats", "reduces anxiety in your child", "ADHD/autism therapy". |
| Trademarks | Social Stories™ is a trademark; "A Little SPOT", "Coco Wyo" are brands. "Telning" is a Swedish dictionary word, so the word alone is weak as a trademark. | Do not use their names or method names on products. File "Telning" together with the logo (word + device mark) in classes 16 and 41 at EUIPO; USPTO later. Check "TADA" and "Pim" in class 16 as well. |
| Legal owner on the copyright page | The copyright line should name the legal owner. "Telning Publishing House" can be used on that line only if it is registered as a secondary business name (särskilt företagsnamn) of WelloWork AB at Bolagsverket. | Register "Telning Publishing House" as a secondary business name, then use it everywhere except Terms & Conditions, where WelloWork AB is named in full. Until then, the copyright line reads "© 2026 WelloWork AB, publishing as Telning". |
| Copyright of AI art | Unsettled in many countries. | We own the method, the story scripts, the character model sheet, the text. Keep human creative records (scripts, sketches, decisions) as evidence of authorship. |
| Medical practice | Individual advice to a named child via a form can look like practice of medicine. | Only general tips; always "ask your paediatrician". No diagnosis, no labels. |

## 14. Risks and answers

| Risk | Answer |
|---|---|
| Claude cannot draw rich art; pages may look plain | Plain is the trend ("bold and easy"). Win on story, interaction and method, not detail. |
| Character drifts between pages | Component-based SVG model sheet; every page built from the same parts. |
| Nobody searches "wordless" | Title uses the parent's problem words; "wordless" goes in the subtitle and keywords. |
| AI-book stigma in reviews | Disclose honestly; quality check every page; human story design; a real team of cognitive and behavioural scientists credited on the back cover. |
| Thin paper complaints | One picture per sheet, blank back, say so on the cover. |
| Health-claim trouble | Honest-claims rule; credentials quiet. |
| Children's data | Minimal data, consent, deletion, separate notice, no analysis. |
| Big publisher copies the idea | Move fast, build the series and the email list; the method and the brand are the moat. |
| Too slow for December 2026 | Accept 2–3 books for December; five by February. |
