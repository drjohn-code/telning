# Telning Research Guideline
## What the evidence says, and what we do with it (working document)

- Version: 1.0, 5 October 2026
- Goes with: `wordless-coloring-stories-project-brief.md` (the brief) and `telning-design-system.md` v2.0
- Where this file and the brief disagree, this file wins. The brief is updated next.
- Decisions made today: age 3–7; a human illustrator draws everything; publish online first (Amazon KDP, worldwide); talk to Swedish publishers after; move fast.

## 1. Summary

### Problem
We want to be the best in the market, fast, in the US and Europe, with Sweden as a possible extra channel. We needed to know three things: what the science supports, what buyers reward and punish, and what the route to market changes.

### Solution
Keep the product idea. Change four things:
1. **The TADA parent guide is the main product.** The evidence says the talk is what helps children, not the book alone.
2. **Age 3–7.** The science and the market both point younger. The pages must be simpler.
3. **Human-made book.** All art and all printed words are made by people. This keeps Amazon clean of AI disclosure and keeps Swedish publishers open.
4. **Amazon first, worldwide, with the Swedish-language rights held back** for publishers for four months.

### Outcome
Book 1 live on Amazon in English by mid-December 2026 if the illustrator is fast, otherwise by 20 January 2027. Swedish manuscript with publishers by January 2027. German edition by February 2027. Honest claims that no reviewer or regulator can attack.

## 3. What the evidence says

Each part ends with "What we use" and "What we do not claim".

### 3.1 Wordless books and talk

| Study | Who | Result | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goriot, Jongstra & Mensink 2024, *L1-Educational Studies in Language and Literature* 24(1), doi:10.21248/l1esll.2024.24.1.712 | Review of 35 peer-reviewed articles, children 0–12 | Wordless books may help speech, language and social-emotional development. Adults talk in ways that support language. | A review with no pooled effect. Many small studies. |
| Chaparro-Moreno, Reali & Maldonado-Carreño 2017, *Early Childhood Research Quarterly* 40, 52–62 | 13 preschool teachers, groups of four children | With wordless books, children said more words, more different words and more sentences. Teachers gave better support and spent longer (17.5 vs 13.3 minutes). | Measures talk, not learning. |
| Petrie, Mayr, Zhao & Montanari 2023, *Journal of Child Language* 50(1) | 36 parents, children 3.5–5.5 | More interaction, more parent prompts, more child contributions with the wordless book. Parents' own vocabulary was richer with the text book. | Each family read only one book type. |
| Murphy et al. 2025, *Journal of Child Language* 52(2) (wordless book vs print book vs play) | 22 parents, children 3–4 | Parents asked more open questions and added more ideas with wordless books. | Very small. One session each. |
| Grolig, Cohrdes, Tiffin-Richards & Schroeder 2020, *Early Childhood Research Quarterly* 51, 191–203 | 201 German preschoolers, mean age 5 years 5 months, cluster-randomised | Dialogic reading with wordless books improved story understanding and vocabulary. Only the inference effect lasted five months. | Effects small and mostly short-term. Run by trained staff, not parents. |

What we use: "No words, so your child tells the story." "Made for talking together." The parent-page questions.
What we do not claim: "Builds language", "boosts vocabulary", "better than normal books".

### 3.2 Dialogic reading (the talk method)

| Meta-analysis | Scope | Effect | What weakens it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mol, Bus, de Jong & Smeets 2008, *Early Education and Development* 19(1) | 16 dialogic reading studies, ages 2–6 | Expressive vocabulary d = .59 (k = 9, n = 322), a moderate effect | Much smaller for 4–5-year-olds and at-risk children |
| Dowdall et al. 2020, *Child Development* 91(2), doi:10.1111/cdev.13225 | 19 RCTs, N = 2,594, ages 1–6, parent training | Expressive language d = 0.41; receptive d = 0.26; parents' book-sharing skill d = 1.01 | Low-intensity programmes gave near-zero effects. Medium-to-high intensity gave d = 0.54. |
| Noble et al. 2019, *Educational Research Review* 28 | All shared-reading interventions | Overall g = 0.194 | Near zero against active control groups (g = 0.028). Follow-up not significant. |
| Barone et al. 2019, *Educational Research and Evaluation* 25(5–6) | 30 home-based RCTs | Only dialogic reading was consistently positive (about g = 0.26). Other types near zero. | Effects fade. Weaker in low-income families. |
| Three-level meta-analysis 2026, *International Journal of Educational Research* (studies 2000–2025) | 64 studies, 260 effect sizes | Overall g = 0.76; RCTs only g = 0.62 | Very mixed studies. Mostly literacy, not feelings. |

Plain words: talking *with* the child helps more than reading *to* the child. The effect is small to medium. It is biggest right after the programme and then shrinks. A single page of advice is the weakest kind of help. So the guide must be short, concrete and repeated: in the book, in the free PDF, in every social post.

Techniques with the best support (Whitehurst):
- **PEER**: Prompt the child; Evaluate the answer; Expand it with one or two words; Repeat the prompt so the child uses the new words.
- **CROWD prompts**: Completion ("Pim feels…"); Recall ("What happened before?"); Open-ended ("What is happening?"); Wh-questions (who, what, where, why); Distancing ("When did you feel like that?").
- **Easy first, then harder.** Grolig et al. asked literal questions ("Who is there?") and then inference questions ("What is Pim thinking?").

What the one-page TADA guide must contain:
1. One sentence on the goal: "You talk with your child. The child tells the story."
2. The four steps, each with three model sentences:
   - **Talk** (child): the child says what they see. The adult waits, then adds one or two words.
   - **Ask** (adult): one easy "who/what" question, then one "how does Pim feel / why" question.
   - **Decide** (child): the child picks an ending and says why. No wrong answer.
   - **Act** (child + adult): "What could you do if you felt like Pim?" Try it once in real life. Draw the ending; send it.
3. "Read it again." Word learning comes from repeat readings.
4. A short "do not" list: do not quiz, do not correct the colouring, do not rush to the right ending.
5. "A few short sessions a week work better than one long one."

What we use: everything above.
What we do not claim: any effect size on the cover. Those come from other programmes, not from our book.

### 3.3 Feelings books and guided talk

| Study | Who | Result | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ni 2026, *Frontiers in Psychology* 17, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1853362 | 97 Chinese children aged 5–6, randomised, 8 weeks, small groups | Big gains in emotion recognition, causes and inference (d = 1.1–1.6) versus a control group that read the same books in routine sessions | One author, two schools, no follow-up. Very large effects in small trials usually shrink. |
| Grazzani & Ornaghi 2011, *Journal of Child Language* 38(5) | 100 preschoolers aged 3–5, two months, 16 stories | Same stories for both groups. Only the group that talked about feelings after the story improved more, especially at age 4. | Researcher-led. |
| Ornaghi, Grazzani, Cherubin, Conte & Piralli 2015, *Social Development* 24 | Preschoolers, six weeks | Better emotion comprehension and prosocial orientation | Short follow-up |
| *Behavioral Sciences* 2025, 15, 1137 | 60 preschoolers aged 4–5, not randomised | Better emotion regulation | Small, not randomised |
| Narrative review 2025, *Frontiers in Psychology*, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1622536 | Review | Reading plus conversation about emotions gives gains; reading alone does not | No pooled effect |

Is it the book or the talk? **The talk.** Grazzani & Ornaghi used the same books in both groups; only the talk group improved more. This is the single most important fact for Telning. The pictures are the stage; the questions are the play.

What we use: "Talk about feelings together." "Questions for grown-ups inside."
What we do not claim: "Improves emotional intelligence", "teaches self-regulation", "reduces tantrums".

### 3.4 "You decide" pages and child choice

No peer-reviewed study tests choose-your-own-ending or child-drawn endings in picture books for ages 3–7. The idea fits nearby evidence (open questions, "what would you do" prompts, inference questions all help), but "children learn more when they choose" is not an evidence-based claim.

What we use: "Two endings to choose from, and a page to draw your own." Present it as a conversation starter and as the thing nobody else has.
What we do not claim: that choosing the ending builds any skill.

### 3.5 Colouring

| Study | Who | Result | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carsley, Heath & Fajnerova 2015, *Journal of Applied School Psychology* 31(3) | 52 children, mean age 10.9 | Anxiety fell before a test after mandala or free colouring | No non-colouring control. Older children. |
| Carsley & Heath 2019, *Journal of Educational Research* 112(2) | About 152 children, grades 4–6 | Test anxiety fell after both kinds of colouring | No passive control. Ages 9–12. |
| Carsley & Heath 2018, *School Psychology International* 39(3) | About 193 grade-8 students | Similar | Teenagers |
| Rose 2020, *Mind, Brain, and Education* | Adolescents | Effects range from large (adults) to very small; one replication failed | Mixed field |
| Mandala colouring before circumcision 2025, *Journal of Pediatric Nursing* | 78 children aged 6–12 | Lower fear, anxiety and pain | Control did nothing. Medical setting. |
| Flett et al. 2017 | Adults | Small mood benefit | Adults only |

Strict verdict for ages 3–7: **no study supports "calms your child" or "reduces anxiety"**. Every anxiety study used children aged 9 or older. Fine-motor benefits come from small, low-quality classroom studies.

What we use: "Hands-on and screen-free." "A calm thing to do together."
What we do not claim: "Calming", "mindfulness colouring", "reduces anxiety", "builds motor skills" (keep this one to the website, softly, as "supports pencil control").

### 3.6 Design evidence

| Evidence | Finding | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Flack & Horst 2018, *Infant and Child Development* 27(1), e2047 | 3.5-year-olds learned fewer words when two pictures were shown at once. Pointing to the right page fixed it. | One picture per sheet, blank back. On the "you decide" spread, add a pointing cue. |
| Fisher, Godwin & Seltman 2014, *Psychological Science* 25(7) | Kindergarten children in a heavily decorated room were more off-task and learned less | One action per page. Few background details. |
| Tare, Chiong, Ganea & DeLoache 2010, *Journal of Applied Developmental Psychology* 31 | Flaps and gimmicks reduced toddlers' learning | No flaps, stickers or extras. |
| Simcock & DeLoache 2006; Ganea et al. | Realistic pictures transfer better to real life; talking animals weaken fact learning | Draw real objects realistically (stethoscope, waiting room). Pim stays stylised. |
| Illustration preference study (older, N = 71) | Children preferred bright, realistic pictures; style did not change comprehension | The cover must be bright. Inside, the child adds the colour. |
| Lowenfeld / Kellogg drawing stages | 2–4 scribbling; 4–7 first symbols, colour not realistic, big shapes | Design the page for the 3-year-old (see 3.8); tell the story for the 5-year-old. |

### 3.7 Autism, speech therapy and Social Stories

- Wordless books are used by speech-language therapists to get children to tell stories and to assess them. Grolig et al. note most wordless-book interventions were with children with language impairment, autism or hearing loss. We did not check individual autism trials.
- **Social Stories** is Carol Gray's trademarked method with strict writing rules. Our books are wordless, so they are not Social Stories. Never use the term on a product.

What we use (blog and teacher pack only): "Speech therapists often use wordless books." "Clear sequence, one change per page, calm pictures."
What we do not claim: "For autistic children", "therapy book", "therapist-approved".

### 3.8 Why age 3–7

Evidence that points younger:
- Dialogic reading effects are largest at ages 2–4 and drop at 4–5 (Mol 2008).
- Emotion-talk gains were biggest at age 4 (Grazzani & Ornaghi 2011).
- The one-picture-per-page study used 3.5-year-olds (Flack & Horst 2018).
- Sweden: books for ages 3–6 grew 30.6% in Q2 2025 and 36.1% in Q3 2025, the fastest segment. First nine months of 2025: +23.8% to 155 million SEK.
- US: *The Big Feelings Coloring Book* (Macmillan) targets 3–5. *Simple & Big Coloring Book for Toddler* has about 7,700 ratings. Amazon's age buckets are 3–5 and 6–8, so "3–7" appears in both.

What age 3 changes:
- A 3-year-old scribbles. They will not colour inside lines. So: lines about 2 mm, very large areas, one character plus one or two things per page, no small details. The picture must still be readable after a scribble.
- The adult does more of the talking at 3 and less at 6. The guide says this.
- "You decide": a 3-year-old points. The adult may colour. Drawing an ending can be a scribble; the story card accepts it.
- Story complexity stays at the 5-year-old level (cause → feeling → try → fix).

Decision: **"Ages 3–7" on every cover.** KDP reading age: minimum 3, maximum 7. Swedish shop category: 3–6 år. Book 4 (doctor) may be aimed 3–6 and book 5 (bedtime) 3–5 if testing shows this.

## 5. What buyers praise and punish

| Buyers punish | Buyers praise | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| Thin paper, markers bleed through (the number one complaint across review analyses) | Thick or one-sided pages | One picture per sheet, blank back. Say it on the cover. Parent page: "Crayons and pencils work best. Put a sheet behind the page if you use markers." |
| Pages printed off-centre or upside down | — | Proof copy before every publish. Never skip. |
| Fewer pages than promised, repeated pages, "feels like a cheap knockoff" | True page count, variety | True picture count on the cover. Rotate composition. |
| AI art | "Hand-drawn", "cute", a character the child knows | "Hand-drawn by [name]" on the cover. Same Pim in every book. |
| Pages too hard, child gives up | "Easy", "my child finishes a page" | Bold-and-easy line rules (design system section 7). |

Market lesson from the research: a feelings colouring book from a big publisher (Castle Point / Macmillan, 2024) had an Amazon rank around 4 million, while self-published series with a character and many titles (A Little SPOT, 56 titles; Coco Wyo, about a quarter of all US colouring books sold in 2026) win. Reviews and a series beat a publisher logo.

## 6. Market

### 6.1 United States (Circana BookScan, about 85% of US print sales)
- Children's activity books: 16.7 million print units in 2025, up 18%, against 3% for all children's books.
- Q1 2026: total print units down 3.1%; only children's books grew (juvenile fiction +5.9%, nonfiction +4.9%).
- Growth is linked to screen-free, activity-based books. SEL (social-emotional learning) is named as a theme by industry writers; it was not confirmed as a sales driver in Circana's own text.
- Coco Wyo format: 52 pages, 8.5 x 8.5 in, one-sided, about 80 gsm paper on Amazon, $9.99.

### 6.2 Sweden (Bokförsäljningsstatistiken, forlaggare.se and booksellers.se)
- 2025: printed children's and young adult books +13.3% in volume and +16.9% in revenue. Foreign-language sales passed 1 billion SEK.
- Q3 2025: children's revenue +34.9% to 227 million SEK; ages 3–6 +36.1% to 59 million SEK.
- Schools and libraries buy a lot of "long tail" titles.
- The state grant for school book purchases (Skolverket, "statsbidrag för inköp av litteratur"): 480 million SEK in 2025; 500 million SEK plus an announced extra 50 million for 2026. For printed fiction and nonfiction, preschool through upper secondary. The school organiser pays at least 20%. Not for public libraries. No central book list; local staff choose. The 2026 request window was 1 September–1 October 2026, so the next chance is the 2027 grant, if it continues.
- Swedish feelings books for ages 3–6 are many (text picture books with "questions to talk about"). Hardbacks sell at 156–229 kr. None is a wordless colouring story.

### 6.3 Competitor map and the gap
- In English, nothing found that combines wordless + colouring + feelings + chosen or drawn ending + parent talk guide. Closest: *Draw Story* (choose-your-own-adventure colouring, with words), *Write Your Own Story Coloring Book* (Dover, 1979), *The Story of Jesus: Wordless Coloring Book* (fixed ending), *A Little Spot Color Fun Book* (complete-the-picture prompts).
- Not yet checked: Swedish, German and Nordic shops; a full Amazon top-15 table (price, pages, ratings, parent prompts) for US, UK and DE. These are tasks in section 10.

### 6.4 Sources not to quote
Market-size reports from paid research sites disagree by billions and hide their methods. Use Circana, Publishers Weekly and the Swedish statistics only.

## 7. Route to market: online first, worldwide, then publishers

### 7.1 Why Amazon first still works with Swedish publishers
It works if four conditions hold:
1. All art and all printed text are human-made, with dated sketches and drafts kept as proof.
2. The Swedish-language edition is **not** published online until publishers have had four months with the manuscript.
3. We tell publishers about the English edition and show its sales.
4. The Pim character, the TADA name and the Telning brand stay owned by WelloWork AB.

Risk to accept: some publishers may still see an online English edition as "already published". The research could not confirm this either way. Sales numbers and reviews reduce the risk.

### 7.2 Languages and editions
- The pictures are wordless. Only the title, the parent page, the feelings-check labels, the QR page and the back cover change.
- Each language is its own KDP title with its own ISBN. Use free ISBNs from Kungliga biblioteket under "Telning Publishing House" (check the KB rules; this makes us the publisher of record in Swedish systems).
- Order: English (all Amazon shops) → German (Amazon.de is the biggest EU shop) → Swedish (Amazon.se, only after the publisher window) → French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Polish when books 1–3 exist.
- The parent page text must be translated by a person, not a machine, if we want the Swedish route open. Keep the translator's name and date.

### 7.3 Paper decision
KDP options for our 8.5 x 8.5 in (large trim) book, US prices, from KDP's own tables:
- **Black ink, white paper, 74–90 gsm:** fixed cost for 24–108 pages, about $2.30–2.85 (check the calculator). One-sided printing of our pictures.
- **Premium colour, white paper, 88–105 gsm:** $1.00 + $0.080 per page = about $5.80 for 60 pages. At $9.99 the royalty would be about $0.19. It needs a $14.99 price ($8.99 − $5.80 = about $3.19 per copy).
- Standard colour is the same thin paper and needs 72 pages, so it is not useful.
- Ink type cannot be changed after publishing.

Decision for book 1: black ink, one-sided, $9.99. This is the Coco Wyo format and it sells. Say "one picture per sheet" on the cover and give the marker tip on the parent page. Test a premium "thick paper" edition later as a separate title, or print through IngramSpark (70 lb paper) for bookshops and libraries (check Ingram's cost sheet and whether Adlibris and Bokus list Ingram titles).

### 7.4 Price per shop
Set every list price at the floor that keeps the 60% royalty: $9.99 (US), €9.99 (DE, FR, ES, IT, NL), £7.99 (UK), 99 SEK (SE), 40 PLN (PL), CAD 13.99, AUD 13.99, JPY 1,000. Amazon adds VAT in EU shops (6% in Sweden, 7% in Germany), so the buyer sees a little more. Check each market in the KDP calculator; large-trim print costs differ by shop.

### 7.5 Amazon AI rules
AI-generated images or text must be disclosed at upload. With a human illustrator and human-written text, we tick "no". Keep the proof: dated sketches, scans, drafts, the illustrator contract.

### 7.6 The Swedish advantage
- Amazon.se is a KDP paperback shop; we can sell in Sweden from day one in SEK.
- Free ISBNs from Kungliga biblioteket; "Telning Publishing House" as publisher of record once the secondary business name is registered.
- 6% VAT on books.
- Access to Swedish preschools and the state grant when a Swedish edition exists in Swedish shop systems (needs a Swedish distributor or Bokinfo listing; check costs).
- Svenska Tecknare contract templates and fee guidance for the illustrator contract.
- Kulturrådet literature support and Bokstart are possible later (check if print-on-demand titles qualify).
- "Scandinavian design" and "made in Sweden" read well with US and German parents.
- EUIPO trademark filing from an EU company.

### 7.7 Swedish publishers (from January 2027)
- Rabén & Sjögren: Swedish only; no AI-generated or AI-assisted text or images; full manuscript; reply in three to four months; a whole picture book may be sent as one low-resolution PDF.
- Bonnier Carlsen, Natur & Kultur, Lilla Piratförlaget accept manuscripts online; illustrations may be attached. Expect similar AI rules. Check Opal, Olika, Idus, Nypon/Vilja, Tukan, Lind & Co before sending.
- Package: the Swedish book as a PDF (pictures + Swedish text pages), a one-page cover letter, the English sales numbers and reviews, the story-card examples, the series plan.
- Ask to keep: English and all non-Nordic rights; the Pim character and trademark; the right to sell PDFs on our website.
- Royalty is shared between author and illustrator; Illustratörcentrum gives 6–25% of the publisher's price as the normal range.
- **Never pay a hybrid house.** Ekström & Garay says most of its books are author-financed; the press reported packages costing 100,000–150,000 SEK. Sveriges Författarförbund has warned about hybrid publishers.

## 9. The illustrator

- Deliverables: Pim model sheet; two more characters; 20–25 story pictures per book; 2–3 "you decide" spreads; feelings-check faces; cover art; spot art for the parent page; story-card frame. Details in the design system, section 17.
- Fees (estimate, from Svenska Tecknare's guidance of about 1,000 SEK per hour and typical reuse rules): a flat fee for book 1 of roughly 40,000–80,000 SEK for 40–80 hours of simple line art, or a lower flat fee plus a royalty share. Agree a lower per-book fee for books 2–5 once the model sheet exists.
- Rights: under Swedish law the illustrator keeps copyright unless the contract transfers it. The contract must give WelloWork AB ownership of the Pim character and the model sheet, an exclusive worldwide all-media licence (or transfer) for all art in the series, and the right to use other illustrators for later books. Pay fairly for this; it is a big grant. Start from Svenska Tecknare's templates and have a Swedish lawyer read it.
- Credit: "Illustrated by [name]" on the back cover and on Amazon. This is also our "hand-drawn" proof.
